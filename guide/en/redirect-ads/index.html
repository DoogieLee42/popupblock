How to Stop Redirect Ads in Safari on iPhone

You tap a link and land somewhere else. You come back from a new tab and the page you were reading is now an ad. You pause a video, and the page reloads and starts from the beginning. These are redirect ads, and Safari's settings alone can't stop them.

Five tricks redirect ads use

  1. Invisible overlay
    A transparent link covers the page. You think you tapped play; you tapped the ad.
  2. Swapped link
    A normal-looking link changes its address the instant you tap it.
  3. Disguised redirect
    A new tab opens on the site's own address, then forwards you to the ad, so it looks like part of the site.
  4. Tab-under
    The new tab shows the page you were on, while your original tab is quietly sent to the ad.
  5. Reload after a block
    If the ad window is blocked, the page reloads to try again, and your video jumps back to the start.

Why Block Pop-ups doesn't help

Safari's Block Pop-ups setting stops windows that open without a tap. All five tricks above happen right after you tap, which is exactly how legitimate sign-in, payment and "open in new tab" actions work. Safari can't tell them apart, so it lets them through.

What you can do without installing anything

  • Close ad tabs without tapping anything on them.
  • If your page turned into an ad, keep using the new tab with your original page and close the old one. Going back often just redirects again.
  • Move the video scrubber in one drag instead of tapping repeatedly; every tap is another chance for an ad.
  • Use the official service for the same content when you can. It's the only guaranteed fix.

Blocking them automatically

To stop these tricks you need something running inside the page that watches behavior: did the tap really hit a link, does the opening address match it, did the original tab move right after a new one opened, did the page reload right after a blocked window.
