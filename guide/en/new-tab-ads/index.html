Why Does Safari Keep Opening New Tabs on My iPhone?

You tap play on a video and a new tab opens with a shopping site, a "You won!" page or an app install screen. You close it, tap again, and it happens again. Is your iPhone infected?

Short answer

Almost always, it's the website, not your iPhone. If it only happens on certain sites, the site has aggressive ad scripts. Run through the checks below; if it keeps happening, a Safari extension can stop it.

Why it happens

Some free video, comic and forum sites lay an invisible layer over the page, or attach hidden handlers to the play button. The moment you tap, the script opens an ad in a new tab.

To Safari, that tab looks like something you opened on purpose, because it opened right after your tap. Sign-in and payment windows work the same way, so Safari's built-in pop-up blocker lets it through. Sneakier sites even open the original page in the new tab and send your old tab to the ad (a "tab-under").

Check these first (free)

  1. Turn on Block Pop-ups
    Settings › Apps › Safari › Block Pop-ups. It stops windows that open without a tap.
  2. Turn on Fraudulent Website Warning
    Same screen. Safari warns you before known phishing pages load.
  3. Don't tap anything on the ad page
    "Virus detected", "You won" and "Install now" pages are ads designed to scare you. Close the tab instead. A web page can't scan or infect your iPhone.
  4. Look for unknown profiles
    Settings › General › VPN & Device Management. Remove any profile you don't recognize.
  5. Review your Safari extensions
    Settings › Apps › Safari › Extensions. Turn off anything you don't remember installing.
  6. Clear website data
    If Safari opens a strange page on launch, use Settings › Apps › Safari › Clear History and Website Data. You'll be signed out of sites.

Still happening?

If the checks come up clean and it only happens on certain sites, the cause is the site's ad script. Safari settings can't tell a disguised ad tab from a real one, so you need an extension that checks, at the moment you tap, whether you actually tapped a link and whether the page opening matches it. A regular banner ad blocker works from lists of ad addresses and often misses these, because the sites keep changing them.
