Tapped an Ad's X and It Opened the Ad? Fake Close Buttons Explained

An ad covers the page, you tap the little X in the corner, and instead of closing, Safari opens a shopping site or an app install page. Your aim was fine. The X was part of the ad.

How fake close buttons work

  • A fake X inside the ad: it looks like a close button, but it's an ad link.
  • A transparent layer over the real X: the real button is underneath; your tap hits the ad first.
  • A tiny X: hard to hit with a finger, so you tap the ad around it.
  • A late X: it appears after a few seconds, exactly where an ad link is already waiting.
  • Close plus a new tab: the ad closes, but an ad site opens in a new tab at the same time.

"The X won't close the ad"

If tapping the X does nothing, check your tab list. Each tap may be opening an ad in another tab. If no tabs open, the X may be just a picture with no close function, and reloading the page is the only way out.

What you can do right now

  1. Wait a few seconds before tapping the X; many move into place after loading.
  2. If an ad tab opens, close it without tapping anything on it.
  3. For article pages, use the page menu › Show Reader to read without ads.

Blocking them automatically

A fake X looks identical to a real one, so the only reliable signal is what happens next: you tapped something that looks like close, and the page tries to leave the site or open a new window. Safari's Block Pop-ups setting allows windows opened right after a tap, so it can't catch this. A Safari extension that watches for it can.
